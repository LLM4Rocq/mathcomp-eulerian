\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About this document}

This document is a Stanley-style reading of the
\href{https://github.com/LLM4Rocq/mathcomp-eulerian}{\texttt{mathcomp-eulerian}}
formalization. Each definition and statement is given twice: once in
ordinary mathematical English (in the spirit of Stanley
\emph{Enumerative Combinatorics I}, Chapter~1) and once as the
verbatim Rocq text, with a clickable link to the line in the
repository on GitHub. The intention is that a mathematician can read
top to bottom like a textbook and at any point click through to
verify the formal statement.

\paragraph{Status.} This edition covers all of Stanley
\S1.3 (cycles, Stirling numbers, inversions, major index, Foata),
\S1.4 (descents, Eulerian numbers, $\beps{n}{S}$, $q$-analogues),
\S1.6.2 (longest alternating subsequence), and \S1.6.3 (toggle
action, $\omega$-map, and the headline Corollary~1.6.5). Stanley
\S1.6.4 (Andr\'e's reflection method) is included as
Chapter~\ref{ch:reflection} but is \emph{partial}: the headline
recurrence is proven modulo one named admit, in a file out of the
active build chain. See that chapter for details and pointers to
the retrospective document.

\paragraph{First-time readers.} Read Chapter~\ref{ch:primer} once,
then Chapters~\ref{ch:cycles}--\ref{ch:reflection} top to bottom.
Use Appendix~\ref{app:glossary} as a quick reference on second
reading. The primer and glossary together contain everything a
Stanley reader needs to know about mathcomp to verify the formal
statements; you do not need to install Rocq.

\paragraph{Auxiliary lemmas.} The narrative chapters cover the
$\sim$70 most-cited results in the formalization. The full library
contains $\sim$830 named results
(Definition/Lemma/Theorem/Corollary), all of which are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:catalog} with their \texttt{coqdoc} docstrings
and clickable links to GitHub. Auxiliary lemmas (those not
narrated in a chapter) are flagged \textsf{[aux]}; narrated entries
carry a \textsf{[Ch~$X$ §$X.Y$]} badge pointing back to where they
appear in the body. The catalog is auto-generated from the
\texttt{.v} files; adding a docstring to a result automatically
enriches both the catalog and the deployed coqdoc HTML.

\paragraph{Conventions.} The single recurring shift is 0-indexing.
Stanley writes $w \in \Sn{n}$ for permutations of $\{1, \dots, n\}$
and $\descent(w) \subseteq \{1, \dots, n-1\}$ for the descent set;
we use $\Perm{\ord{n}}$ for permutations of
$\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ and $\Set{\ord{n-1}}$ for the descent set.
Concretely:
\[
  \text{our } \Perm{\ord{n+1}} \;=\; \text{Stanley's } \Sn{n+1}.
\]
A complete translation table is in
\href{https://github.com/LLM4Rocq/mathcomp-eulerian/blob/main/docs/READING_GUIDE.md}{\texttt{docs/READING\_GUIDE.md}};
a worked example through Stanley's $w = [3,1,4,2]$ is in the same file.

\paragraph{Reading the formal text.} Each Rocq block looks like
this:

\rocqsource{descent.v}{25}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition descent_set s : {set 'I_n} := [set i | is_descent s i].
\end{lstlisting}
\decoded{$\descent(s) = \{\,i \in \{0, \dots, n-1\} : s(i) > s(i+1)\,\}$.}

\noindent The bracketed link at the top is clickable and opens the
exact line on GitHub. The Rocq body is verbatim; identifiers and
keywords are highlighted but no mathematical substitution has been
performed. The shaded \emph{Decoded} line below the block restates
the formal text in plain mathematics, mechanically substituting
mathcomp casts for their meaning. Side by side, the two should say
the same thing; if they do not, that is exactly the kind of
discrepancy worth flagging.

\noindent Some definitions also carry a \textsf{Design choice}
sidebar that explains why a particular formalization was chosen
when a more natural-looking alternative exists. These boxes are
for first reading and can be skipped on a re-read.

\paragraph{What is \emph{not} included here.}
Full proofs are not included; only the formal \emph{statements} are
shown alongside the informal mathematics, with a one-paragraph proof
sketch for each non-trivial result. The full proof is always one
click away in the linked source. The cd-index development
(\texttt{mmtree.v}, \texttt{psi\_*.v}) is project-internal scaffolding
not in Stanley and is not covered.

\paragraph{Verifying axiom-freeness.} Every theorem in this document
is kernel-validated by Rocq with no axioms or admits. To check this

\chapter{A combinatorialist's mathcomp primer}
\label{ch:primer}
% =====================================================================

This chapter is a focused reference for the few mathcomp primitives
that appear in Chapter~\ref{ch:descents}. It is not a tutorial; it
gives just enough vocabulary to read the formal blocks and recognise
that they say what the informal text claims. Skim it once on first
read; come back to it as a glossary when re-reading.

% =====================================================================
\section{Ordinals: the type \texttt{'I\_n}}
% =====================================================================

The type \texttt{'I\_n} (read ``I-sub-n'') is mathcomp's
\emph{finite type} of natural numbers strictly less than $n$. An
inhabitant is a pair: a natural number \texttt{i : nat} and a
\emph{proof} \texttt{i\,<\,n}. The two together are written
\texttt{Ordinal i (proof : i\,<\,n)} but a reader normally never
constructs ordinals by hand --- the type appears as the index of a
sum or a permutation domain, and inhabitants come from the typing
context.

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{ord0 : 'I\_n.+1}] the value $0$, with its proof
    $0 < n+1$. Always available when the bound is at least $1$.
  \item[\texttt{ord\_max : 'I\_n.+1}] the value $n$, with its proof.
    The largest element of \texttt{'I\_n.+1}.
  \item[\texttt{val i : nat}] strips an ordinal back to its
    underlying natural number, forgetting the proof.
\end{description}

\noindent The \emph{cardinality} of \texttt{'I\_n} is exactly $n$
(lemma \texttt{card\_ord} in \texttt{fintype.v}). So
\texttt{\#|'I\_4|\,=\,4}.

% =====================================================================
\section{Casts: \texttt{widen\_ord}, \texttt{lift}, \texttt{unlift}}
\label{sec:casts}
% =====================================================================

There are exactly two natural maps $\ord{n} \to \ord{n+1}$:

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{widen\_ord (h : n \,\textbackslash leq\, n+1) i}]
    sends \texttt{i : 'I\_n} to the same numerical value, viewed
    inside the bigger type. Diagrammatically:
    \begin{center}
      $i : \ord{n} \quad\xrightarrow{\;\texttt{widen\_ord}\;}\quad i : \ord{n+1}.$
    \end{center}
    The $h$ argument is a proof that $n \leq n+1$, supplied by
    \texttt{leqnSn n}. Mathcomp users write
    \texttt{widen\_ord (leqnSn n) i} reflexively.

  \item[\texttt{lift (p : 'I\_n.+1) i}]
    sends \texttt{i : 'I\_n} to a value in \texttt{'I\_n.+1}
    \emph{skipping} the value $p$. With $p = \texttt{ord0}$, this
    shifts every value up by one:
    \begin{center}
      $i : \ord{n} \quad\xrightarrow{\;\texttt{lift ord0}\;}\quad i+1 : \ord{n+1}.$
    \end{center}
    The general \texttt{lift p i} skips $p$, which is exactly what
    one needs when removing one position from a permutation.
\end{description}

\noindent The two casts together name two specific elements of
\texttt{'I\_n.+1} from the same source $i : \ord{n}$: the value $i$
itself, and the value $i+1$. This is the idiom that lets us write
``the comparison between consecutive positions $i$ and $i+1$''
without ever leaving the well-typed world of finite ordinals.

The partial inverse of \texttt{lift p} is \texttt{unlift p}, of
type \texttt{'I\_n.+1\,\textbackslash to\,option 'I\_n}. Concretely:
\texttt{unlift p i = None} if \texttt{i = p}, and
\texttt{unlift p i = Some j} otherwise, with $j$ the unique witness
of \texttt{i = lift p j}. This is the canonical Rocq idiom for the
case-split ``is the input the special index $p$, or one of the
others?'' --- it appears in every inductive bijection on ordinals.

% =====================================================================
\section{Finite sets: \texttt{\{set T\}}}
% =====================================================================

For \texttt{T} a finite type (such as \texttt{'I\_n}),
\texttt{\{set T\}} is the type of \emph{finite subsets of T}, with
\emph{extensional} equality: two sets are equal iff they have the
same elements.

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{[set i : T\ |\ P i]}] set comprehension --- the set
    of \texttt{i : T} satisfying the boolean predicate $P$. The
    vertical bar reads as ``such that'', as in mathematical notation.
  \item[\texttt{\#|S|}] the cardinality of \texttt{S} as a natural
    number.
  \item[\texttt{i \textbackslash in S}] membership test, returning
    a \texttt{bool}.
  \item[\texttt{\textasciitilde:\,S}] complement, \texttt{S\,:|:\,T}
    union, \texttt{S\,:\&:\,T} intersection, \texttt{S\,:\textbackslash:\,T}
    difference.
\end{description}

\noindent \texttt{\{set T\}} is itself a finite type, which means
big operators (sums, products) can be indexed by sets. This matters
for the Eulerian recurrence and for the partition-by-descent-set
identity \texttt{beta\_eulerian}.

% =====================================================================
\section{Permutations: \texttt{\{perm T\}}}
% =====================================================================

\texttt{\{perm T\}} is the type of \emph{bijections} \texttt{T \textbackslash to T}
for \texttt{T} a finite type. An inhabitant is a function
together with a proof of bijectivity, packaged so that:

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{s i}] applies the permutation \texttt{s} to
    \texttt{i : T} as a function. (No special syntax is needed:
    \texttt{\{perm T\}} \emph{coerces} to a function.)
  \item[\texttt{1\%g}] is the identity permutation. The \texttt{\%g}
    selects mathcomp's group scope.
  \item[\texttt{(s * t)\%g i}] is composition. Mathcomp's convention
    is that \texttt{(s * t) i = t (s i)} (right-to-left for the
    \emph{action}; the lemma is \texttt{permM}).
  \item[\texttt{perm (h : injective f)}] constructs a permutation
    from an injective function \texttt{f : T \textbackslash to T} (on a finite
    type, injective implies bijective).
\end{description}

\noindent The cardinality of \texttt{\{perm 'I\_n\}} is $n!$
(Stanley's $|\Sn{n}|$); this is the lemma \texttt{card\_perm} or
\texttt{card\_Sn} in mathcomp.

% =====================================================================
\section{Big operators: \texttt{\textbackslash sum}, \texttt{\textbackslash prod}}
% =====================================================================

Mathcomp's \texttt{bigop.v} provides indexed sums and products with
a uniform syntax:

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\ :\ T) f i}] sums \texttt{f i}
    over every \texttt{i : T} (\texttt{T} a finite type).
  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\ <\ n) f i}] same, with
    \texttt{i : 'I\_n}.
  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\ \textbackslash in\ S) f i}]
    sums over an explicit set \texttt{S}.
  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\ |\ P i) f i}] sums over
    indices satisfying the boolean filter \texttt{P}.
\end{description}

\noindent The vertical bar in \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\ |\ P i)}
is a \emph{filter}, not the disjunction symbol. Replace
\texttt{\textbackslash sum} by \texttt{\textbackslash prod} for
products. Several recurring \emph{moves} appear in the proofs:

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{partition\_big f xpredT}] rewrite
    \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_x g x} as
    \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_y \textbackslash sum\_(x\ |\ f x = y) g x}
    --- partition by the value of $f$.
  \item[\texttt{bigID p}] split a sum into the part where the
    predicate $p$ holds and the part where it does not.
  \item[\texttt{reindex \ldots}] change of summation variable along
    a bijection.
\end{description}

\noindent These names appear in the proofs we will not reprint in
this document, but they are worth recognising if you click through
to a \texttt{.v} file.

% =====================================================================
\section{Sections and section variables}
% =====================================================================

Every formalization file in this project opens with

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Section X.
Variable n : nat.
Definition foo := ...    (* implicitly takes n *)
Lemma bar : ...          (* implicitly takes n *)
End X.
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Inside the section, definitions and lemmas may use
\texttt{n} as if it were globally fixed. At \texttt{End X}, every
definition and lemma is automatically generalised to take \texttt{n}
as an explicit argument. This is why \texttt{descent.v} reads
\texttt{Variable n : nat} once at the top and then
\texttt{Definition is\_descent s i := ...} without an explicit
\texttt{n}, but outside the file \texttt{is\_descent} is invoked as
\texttt{is\_descent n s i}. (For the mathematician: think of
\texttt{Variable} as ``Let \texttt{n} be a natural number'' at the
start of a chapter.)

% =====================================================================
\section{The takeaway}
% =====================================================================

Three points are enough to read Chapter~\ref{ch:descents}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{\{perm 'I\_n.+1\}} is mathcomp's $\Sn{n+1}$. Apply
    a permutation to a position by writing \texttt{s i}; the descent
    set is a \texttt{\{set 'I\_n\}} (positions, not values).
  \item The two casts \texttt{widen\_ord (leqnSn n) i} and
    \texttt{lift ord0 i} pick out positions $i$ and $i+1$ inside
    \texttt{'I\_n.+1} from a single $i : \ord{n}$. The reader does
    not need to verify cast arithmetic by hand --- the kernel
    rejects the file unless every cast is the unique one of its
    type.
  \item Big operators \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(\ldots)} are
    just sums; the syntax variants control the index range. The
    vertical bar is a filter.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent With these in hand, the formal blocks in
Chapter~\ref{ch:descents} read directly. Each formal block is
followed by a one-line \emph{Decoded} reading that mechanically
substitutes the mathcomp casts for their mathematical meaning, so
the reader has both forms side by side.

\chapter{Cycles and Stirling numbers}
\label{ch:cycles}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes Stanley \S1.3.1 (cycle representation of
permutations) and \S1.3.2 (Stirling numbers of the first kind). The
two main objects are the cycle count $c(w)$ and the (signless)
Stirling cycle number $\stirling{n}{k}$ that counts permutations by
$c(w)$. They are the cycle-side analogues of $\des(w)$ and
$\eulerianA(n, k)$ from Chapter~\ref{ch:descents}.

% =====================================================================
\section{Cycle count}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Cycle decomposition, cycle count]
  \label{def:cycle_count}
  Every $w \in \Sn{n}$ admits a unique decomposition into a set of
  \emph{disjoint cycles}; let $c(w)$ denote the number of such
  cycles.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{cycles.v}{24}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition cycle_count {T : finType} (s : {perm T}) : nat := #|porbits s|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$c(s) = $ number of orbits of the action of the cyclic
group $\langle s \rangle$ on the underlying set --- that is, the
number of cycles in the disjoint-cycle decomposition.}

\begin{sidebar}{why \texttt{porbits}, not a manual cycle decomposition?}
A naive formalization would build the cycle decomposition by hand:
pick an element, follow the orbit, mark seen, repeat. Mathcomp's
\texttt{porbits} (in \texttt{perm.v}) does this once and exposes the
result as a \texttt{\{set \{set T\}\}} --- a set of orbits ---
together with all the lemmas one would want
(\texttt{porbits\_partition}, \texttt{cover\_porbits}, etc.). Reusing
it costs nothing and gives proofs like \texttt{cycle\_count\_id}
in three lines instead of thirty. The cardinality of the orbit set
is exactly the cycle count.
\end{sidebar}

\begin{lemma}[Cycle count bound]
  \label{lem:cycle_count_le}
  $c(w) \leq |T|$ for every $w \in \Sn{T}$, with equality iff $w$
  is the identity.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{cycles.v}{44}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma cycle_count_le {T : finType} (s : {perm T}) :
  cycle_count s <= #|T|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The number of cycles is at most the number of elements
(each cycle has at least one element).}

\rocqsource{cycles.v}{64}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma cycle_count_id (T : finType) :
  cycle_count (1 : {perm T}) = #|T|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The identity has every element as a fixed point, i.e.\ a
1-cycle; the cycle count equals $|T|$.}

% =====================================================================
\section{Stirling cycle numbers}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Signless Stirling cycle numbers]
  \label{def:stirling_c}
  The number $\stirling{n}{k}$ is the count of permutations of
  $[n] = \{1, \dots, n\}$ with exactly $k$ cycles.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{cycles.v}{33}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition stirling_c (n k : nat) : nat :=
  #|[set s : {perm 'I_n} | cycle_count s == k]|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\stirling{n}{k} = |\{s \in \Sn{n} : c(s) = k\}|$, the
number of perms of length $n$ with $k$ cycles. \emph{Direct match
to Stanley} (no off-by-one): we use \texttt{\{perm 'I\_n\}}, of
size $n$, exactly mirroring Stanley's $S_n$.}

\begin{lemma}[Row sum recovers $n!$]
  \label{lem:stirling_c_row_sum_fact}
  $\sum_{k=0}^{n} \stirling{n}{k} = n!$
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{cycles.v}{98}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma stirling_c_row_sum_fact n :
  \sum_(k < n.+1) stirling_c n k = n`!.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Sum over $k \in \{0, \dots, n\}$ of $\stirling{n}{k}$
equals $n!$, since the sets partition $\Sn{n}$ by cycle count.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} The sets $\{s : c(s) = k\}$ for $k$ ranging
over all possible cycle counts partition $\Sn{n}$. Total
cardinality is $|\Sn{n}| = n!$. \qed

\begin{theorem}[Stirling cycle recurrence]
  \label{thm:stirling_c_rec}
  For $n, k \geq 0$,
  \[
    \stirling{n+1}{k+1} \;=\; n \cdot \stirling{n}{k+1} \;+\; \stirling{n}{k}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{stirling_fiber.v}{341}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma stirling_c_rec n k :
  stirling_c n.+1 k.+1 = n * stirling_c n k.+1 + stirling_c n k.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Stanley's recurrence for the signless Stirling cycle
numbers: a permutation of $[n+1]$ with $k+1$ cycles either inserts
the element $n+1$ into one of the existing $n$ cycle-positions of
a perm of $[n]$ with $k+1$ cycles ($n$ choices), or sits as a
new fixed point in a perm of $[n]$ with $k$ cycles.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Partition perms of $[n+1]$ with $k+1$ cycles
by whether $n+1$ is a fixed point. If yes, the rest is a perm of
$[n]$ with $k$ cycles (giving $\stirling{n}{k}$). If no, $n+1$
sits inside an existing cycle; there are $n$ positions to insert
it, giving $n \cdot \stirling{n}{k+1}$.

The formal proof uses an auxiliary construction
\texttt{insert\_after} (in \texttt{stirling\_fiber.v}) that
realises the bijection
$[n] \times \{s \in \Sn{n} : c(s) = k+1\} \to
\{\sigma \in \Sn{n+1} : \sigma(n+1) \neq n+1,\ c(\sigma) = k+1\}$. \qed

% =====================================================================
\chapter{Inversions and the major index}
\label{ch:inversions}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes Stanley \S1.3.3 (the inversion count $\inv$
and the major index $\maj$, the two classical Mahonian statistics)
and the basic identities each satisfies. The headline result
--- their equidistribution over $\Sn{n}$ --- requires the Foata
bijection and is the subject of Chapter~\ref{ch:foata}.

% =====================================================================
\section{Inversions}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Inversion]
  \label{def:is_inv}
  A pair $(i, j)$ with $1 \leq i < j \leq n$ is an
  \emph{inversion} of $w \in \Sn{n}$ if $w_i > w_j$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{26}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition is_inv s (i j : 'I_n.+1) : bool :=
  (i < j) && (s j < s i).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$(i, j)$ is an inversion of $s$ iff $i < j$ and $s(j) < s(i)$
(i.e.\ $s(i) > s(j)$).}

\begin{definition}[Inversion set, inversion count]
  \label{def:inv}
  $\mathrm{Inv}(w) = \{(i, j) : i < j,\ w_i > w_j\}$, and
  $\inv(w) = |\mathrm{Inv}(w)|$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{30}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition inv_set s : {set 'I_n.+1 * 'I_n.+1} :=
  [set ij | is_inv s ij.1 ij.2].
\end{lstlisting}
\decoded{The set of inversion pairs.}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{34}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition inv s : nat := #|inv_set s|.
\end{lstlisting}
\decoded{$\inv(s)$ is the cardinality of the inversion set.}

\begin{lemma}[Identity has no inversions]
  $\inv(\mathrm{id}) = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{41}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma inv_id : inv (1 : {perm 'I_n.+1}) = 0.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The identity permutation has no $(i, j)$ with $s(i) > s(j)$,
hence no inversions.}

\begin{lemma}[Inversion bound]
  $\inv(w) \leq \binom{n}{2}$ for every $w \in \Sn{n}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{136}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma inv_le s : inv s <= 'C(n.+1, 2).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\inv(s) \leq \binom{n+1}{2}$, the number of pairs
$(i, j)$ with $i < j$ in $\Sn{n+1}$.}

\begin{lemma}[Reversal identity for $\inv$]
  \label{lem:inv_rev_perm}
  $\inv(w^{\mathrm{rev}}) = \binom{n}{2} - \inv(w)$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{219}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma inv_rev_perm s :
  inv (rev_perm s) = 'C(n.+1, 2) - inv s.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The reverse permutation has the ``opposite'' inversion
count: each pair $(i, j)$ with $i < j$ is either an inversion of
$s$ or of its reverse, but not both. Hence the two counts sum to
$\binom{n+1}{2}$.}

% =====================================================================
\section{The major index}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Major index]
  \label{def:maj}
  The \emph{major index} of $w \in \Sn{n}$ is the sum of (1-indexed)
  descent positions:
  \[
    \maj(w) \;=\; \sum_{i \in \descent(w)} i.
  \]
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{81}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition maj s : nat := \sum_(i in descent_set s) (val i).+1.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\maj(s) = \sum_{i \in \descent(s)} (i+1)$. The
\texttt{(val i).+1} adds 1 to recover Stanley's 1-indexing from
our 0-indexed descent positions.}

\begin{sidebar}{why \texttt{(val i).+1} inside the sum, not in the descent set?}
We could ``correct'' the indexing once for all by storing
1-indexed positions in the descent set. We don't, because the
descent set type \texttt{\{set 'I\_n\}} would no longer fit the
ambient type system: 1-indexed positions in $\{1, \dots, n-1\}$
would need \texttt{\{set 'I\_n.-1\}} with values shifted by 1, and
every set-level lemma (complement, partition, big-op) would carry
a translation. Doing the index shift inside \texttt{maj} keeps the
descent-set machinery clean and isolates the translation to the
one definition that needs it. The price is a single
\texttt{(val i).+1} in the formula; the benefit is that
\texttt{descent\_set}, \texttt{beta}, and \texttt{set\_is\_alt}
all live in the same type without conversion casts.
\end{sidebar}

\begin{lemma}[Major-index bound]
  $\maj(w) \leq \binom{n}{2}$ for every $w \in \Sn{n}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{inversions.v}{158}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma maj_le s : maj s <= 'C(n.+1, 2).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\maj(s) \leq \binom{n+1}{2}$, attained by the reverse
identity.}

\begin{example}[{Stanley's $w = [3,1,4,2]$, statistics}]
  Take $w = [3,1,4,2] \in \Sn{4}$ as in Example~\ref{ex:s3142}.
  Pairs $(i, j)$ with $w_i > w_j$: $(1,2)$ since $3 > 1$, $(1,4)$
  since $3 > 2$, $(3,4)$ since $4 > 2$. Hence $\inv(w) = 3$.
  Descent positions: $\descent(w) = \{1, 3\}$, so
  $\maj(w) = 1 + 3 = 4$. In our types,
  $\inv(s) = 3$ and $\maj(s) = (0+1) + (2+1) = 4$, matching.
\end{example}

% =====================================================================
\chapter{Foata's bijection and MacMahon's equidistribution}
\label{ch:foata}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes Stanley \S1.3.4: Foata's first fundamental
bijection on $\Sn{n}$, which sends permutations of major index $k$
bijectively to permutations of inversion count $k$. The corollary is
MacMahon's equidistribution theorem.

% =====================================================================
\section{The Foata transformation on words}
% =====================================================================

The bijection is built from a recursive construction on words. Given
a word $u$ and a new letter $a$, classify the previous letters of
$u$ according to whether they are $< a$ or $> a$ (depending on the
last letter of $u$), split $u$ into blocks at the classifying
letters, and cyclically rotate each block (last $\to$ front). Then
append $a$.

\rocqsourcerange{foata.v}{70}{77}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition foata_step (a : nat) (u : seq nat) : seq nat :=
  match u with
  | [::] => [:: a]
  | _ :: _ =>
      let x := last 0 u in
      let P := if x < a then (fun y : nat => y < a) else (fun y => a < y) in
      flatten (map cyc_last_to_front (split_blocks P u)) ++ [:: a]
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Empty word: just $[a]$. Otherwise: pick the predicate $P$
(``$<a$'' or ``$>a$'' depending on the sign of $\mathrm{last}(u)$
vs $a$), \texttt{split\_blocks P u} cuts $u$ into blocks ending at
$P$-letters, \texttt{cyc\_last\_to\_front} rotates each block (last
letter to front), \texttt{flatten} concatenates, then append $a$.}

\rocqsource{foata.v}{80}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition foata (w : seq nat) : seq nat :=
  foldl (fun u a => foata_step a u) [::] w.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Process $w$ left-to-right, applying \texttt{foata\_step} at
each new letter starting from the empty word.}

\begin{example}[Stanley's running example]
  $w = [3,1,4,5,9,2,6]$. Then:
  \[
    \mathrm{foata}(w) \;=\; [3, 4, 1, 5, 2, 9, 6].
  \]
  Verified inside \texttt{foata.v} (lemma \texttt{sanity\_inv\_eq\_maj}
  at line 125 confirms by \texttt{Compute}). Stanley's hand
  computation on p.~24 gives the same value.
\end{example}

% =====================================================================
\section{Lifting to permutations}
% =====================================================================

The seq-level bijection is wrapped into a permutation via
\texttt{seq\_to\_perm}, which packages a uniqueness-and-bound
proof obligation into a \texttt{\{perm 'I\_n.+1\}}.

\rocqsource{foata.v}{1529}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition foata_perm (s : {perm 'I_n.+1}) : {perm 'I_n.+1} :=
  seq_to_perm (foata_perm_to_seq_size s) (foata_perm_to_seq_uniq s)
              (foata_perm_to_seq_bnd s).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\mathtt{foata\_perm}\,s$ is the permutation whose one-line
sequence is $\mathrm{foata}$ applied to the one-line sequence of $s$.
The three named lemmas discharge the side-conditions
(size, distinctness, bound) needed to package a \texttt{seq nat}
back into a \texttt{\{perm\}}.}

\begin{sidebar}{why a seq-level bijection lifted to perms?}
Foata's bijection is defined inductively on the word (seq), not on
the permutation as a function. Trying to define it directly on
\texttt{\{perm 'I\_n.+1\}} would force every step to re-establish
``the result is a permutation,'' an obligation that the seq-level
construction discharges once at the end. We pay for the wrapping
once (in \texttt{seq\_to\_perm}); in exchange the bijection itself
has the natural, recursive form Stanley describes.
\end{sidebar}

% =====================================================================
\section{The equidistribution theorem}
% =====================================================================

\begin{theorem}[$\inv(\mathrm{foata}\,w) = \maj(w)$]
  \label{thm:foata_perm_inv_maj}
  For every $w \in \Sn{n}$,
  \[
    \inv(\mathrm{foata}(w)) \;=\; \maj(w).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{foata.v}{1576}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma foata_perm_inv_maj n (s : {perm 'I_n.+1}) :
  inv (foata_perm s) = maj s.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The Foata transformation converts $\maj$-statistics to
$\inv$-statistics: for every $s$, the inversion count of
$\mathrm{foata}(s)$ equals the major index of $s$.}

\begin{lemma}[$\mathtt{foata\_perm}$ is injective]
  The map $s \mapsto \mathrm{foata\_perm}\,s$ is injective on
  $\Sn{n+1}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{foata.v}{1586}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma foata_perm_inj n : injective (@foata_perm n).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Different inputs give different outputs (proven by
constructing an explicit inverse \texttt{foata\_inv} at the seq
level).}

\begin{theorem}[MacMahon's equidistribution]
  \label{thm:inv_maj_equidistr}
  For every $n, k$, the number of permutations of $\Sn{n}$ with
  $\inv = k$ equals the number with $\maj = k$:
  \[
    \#\{w \in \Sn{n} : \inv(w) = k\}
    \;=\;
    \#\{w \in \Sn{n} : \maj(w) = k\}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{foata.v}{1598}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem inv_maj_equidistr n k :
  #|[set s : {perm 'I_n.+1} | inv s == k]|
  = #|[set s : {perm 'I_n.+1} | maj s == k]|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The two count functions $\inv$ and $\maj$ have the same
distribution over $\Sn{n+1}$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} The map $s \mapsto \mathrm{foata\_perm}\,s$
sends $\{s : \maj(s) = k\}$ \emph{into} $\{t : \inv(t) = k\}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:foata_perm_inv_maj}); it is injective; on a
finite set, injective implies surjective. So the two sets are in
bijection, hence have equal cardinality. \qed

\chapter{Descents and Eulerian numbers}
\label{ch:descents}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes Stanley \S1.4 (Stanley
\emph{Enumerative Combinatorics I}, 2nd ed., pp.~30--38). The central
objects are the \emph{descent set} of a permutation, the
\emph{Eulerian numbers} $\eulerianA(n, k)$ that count permutations by
descent count, and the set-refined count $\beps{n}{S}$ that counts
permutations by their full descent set.

% =====================================================================
\section{The descent set}
\label{sec:descent_set}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Descent]
  \label{def:is_descent}
  Let $w = (w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n) \in \Sn{n}$. A position
  $i \in \{1, \dots, n-1\}$ is a \emph{descent} of $w$ if
  $w_i > w_{i+1}$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{22}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition is_descent s i : bool :=
  s (widen_ord (leqnSn n) i) > s (lift ord0 i).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$s(i) > s(i+1)$ for $i \in \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ and
$s : \{0, \dots, n\} \to \{0, \dots, n\}$ a bijection.}

\begin{sidebar}{why \texttt{widen\_ord} and \texttt{lift}, not \texttt{s i > s i.+1}?}
The naive form \texttt{s i > s i.+1} does not type-check. The
permutation \texttt{s : \{perm 'I\_n.+1\}} expects an argument of
type \texttt{'I\_n.+1}, but a descent slot $i$ lives in
\texttt{'I\_n} (there are $n$ adjacencies for $n+1$ positions).
The two casts \texttt{widen\_ord (leqnSn n) i} and
\texttt{lift ord0 i} are the canonical maps that ship $i$ into the
larger type \texttt{'I\_n.+1} as the values $i$ and $i+1$
respectively (Primer~\ref{sec:casts}). There is no way to ``just
cast'' --- the two casts encode \emph{which} element of
\texttt{'I\_n.+1} we mean. The verbosity is what makes the
formalization correct by typing.
\end{sidebar}

\noindent The result type \texttt{bool} (rather than \texttt{Prop})
makes \texttt{is\_descent} computable: \texttt{Compute is\_descent s i}
returns \texttt{true} or \texttt{false} for any concrete \texttt{s}
and \texttt{i}. Mathcomp's small-scale reflection bridges
\texttt{is\_descent\,s\,i\,=\,true} with the corresponding
proposition on demand.

\begin{definition}[Descent set]
  \label{def:descent_set}
  The \emph{descent set} of $w \in \Sn{n}$ is
  \[
    \descent(w) \;=\; \{\, i \in \{1, \dots, n-1\} : w_i > w_{i+1} \,\}.
  \]
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{25}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition descent_set s : {set 'I_n} := [set i | is_descent s i].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\descent(s) = \{\,i \in \{0, \dots, n-1\} : s(i) > s(i+1)\,\}$.}

\begin{sidebar}{why \texttt{\{set 'I\_n\}}, not \texttt{pred 'I\_n} or \texttt{seq 'I\_n}?}
Three options exist for representing a finite collection of
positions: a boolean predicate (\texttt{pred T}), a list
(\texttt{seq T}), or a set (\texttt{\{set T\}}).
\texttt{\{set\,'I\_n\}} is the right choice here for two reasons.
First, it has \emph{extensional equality}: two sets are equal iff
they have the same elements, so \texttt{descent\_set s = D} is the
condition we want, with no axiom of function extensionality
required. Second, \texttt{\{set 'I\_n\}} is itself a finite type,
which means we can sum over it with mathcomp's big operators ---
the lemma \texttt{beta\_eulerian} below uses
\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(D : \{set 'I\_n\}\,|\,\#|D|\,==\,k)}
exactly because \texttt{\{set\}} supports this.
\texttt{pred 'I\_n} fails the first test (extensional equality of
predicates needs \texttt{funext}); \texttt{seq 'I\_n} carries
spurious order and multiplicity.
\end{sidebar}

\noindent
$\descent(w)$ is a subset of $\{1, \dots, n-1\}$ in Stanley; in our
types it is a subset of $\{0, \dots, n-1\} = \ord{n}$. The
correspondence is $\text{Stanley's } i \leftrightarrow \text{our } (i-1)$.

\begin{example}[{Stanley's $w = [3,1,4,2]$, both ways}]
  \label{ex:s3142}
  Take $w = [3, 1, 4, 2] \in \Sn{4}$. Then
  $\descent(w) = \{1, 3\}$ since $w_1 = 3 > 1 = w_2$ and
  $w_3 = 4 > 2 = w_4$. In our types, the corresponding
  $s : \Perm{\ord{4}}$ has $s\,0 = 2$, $s\,1 = 0$, $s\,2 = 3$,
  $s\,3 = 1$ (values shifted to $\{0,1,2,3\}$). Then:
  \begin{computestrip}
Compute is_descent s 0.    (* = true, since s 0 = 2 > 0 = s 1 *)
Compute is_descent s 1.    (* = false, since s 1 = 0 < 3 = s 2 *)
Compute is_descent s 2.    (* = true, since s 2 = 3 > 1 = s 3 *)
Compute descent_set s.     (* = [set 0; 2] : {set 'I_3} *)
Compute des s.             (* = 2 *)
  \end{computestrip}
  \noindent After the index shift our $\{0, 2\}$ corresponds to
  Stanley's $\{1, 3\}$, and $\des(s) = 2 = \des(w)$. The
  computation is what the kernel does on the elaborated term ---
  the formal definition matches the informal one not by editorial
  promise but by the test running here on the page.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Descent number, ascent number]
  \label{def:des_asc}
  $\des(w) = |\descent(w)|$ and $\asc(w) = (n-1) - \des(w)$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{27}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition des s : nat := #|descent_set s|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\des(s) = |\descent(s)|$, the number of descent positions.}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{29}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition asc s : nat := n - des s.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\asc(s) = n - \des(s)$, the number of ascent positions
(here $n$ is the number of descent slots, i.e.\ Stanley's $n-1$).}

\begin{sidebar}{why is the parameter \texttt{n} ``perm size minus one''?}
A permutation of $n+1$ elements has $n$ adjacencies, so the
descent set lives in $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ regardless of how we
parameterise. Two choices: name the parameter after the perm size
(forcing every descent-set type to be \texttt{\{set 'I\_n.-1\}}
with a \texttt{.-1}), or name it after the descent slot count
(forcing the perm type to be \texttt{\{perm 'I\_n.+1\}} with a
\texttt{.+1}). We picked the latter because it removes a
\texttt{.-1} from every theorem about descent sets and from every
\texttt{eulerian n k} statement. The cost is a single conversion:
\textbf{our parameter \texttt{n} is Stanley's $n-1$}, and our
\texttt{eulerian\,n\,k} is Stanley's $\eulerianA(n+1, k)$. This
is a project convention, not a mathcomp requirement; mathcomp
itself uses both styles in different files. We flag the off-by-one
explicitly at every theorem statement that crosses it.
\end{sidebar}

\begin{lemma}[Descent count is bounded]
  \label{lem:des_le}
  $\des(w) \leq n - 1$ for every $w \in \Sn{n}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{38}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma des_le s : des s <= n.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\des(s) \leq n$ for every $s : \Perm{\ord{n+1}}$
(equivalently $\des(w) \leq |w| - 1$).}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} $\descent(w) \subseteq \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ which
has $n$ elements; the cardinality is at most $n$. \qed

\begin{lemma}[Identity has no descents]
  \label{lem:des_id}
  $\des(\mathrm{id}) = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{44}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma des_id : des (1 : {perm 'I_n.+1}) = 0.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\des(\mathrm{id}) = 0$, where \texttt{1\%g} is the group
identity, i.e.\ the identity permutation.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} For the identity, $s\,i = i$ at every position,
so $s(i) = i < i+1 = s(i+1)$ --- no descent slot. \qed

% =====================================================================
\section{Reversal symmetry}
\label{sec:reversal}
% =====================================================================

A useful symmetry: reversing a permutation flips ascents and
descents at corresponding positions.

\begin{definition}[Reverse permutation]
  \label{def:rev_perm}
  Let $w \in \Sn{n}$ and define $w^{\mathrm{rev}}$ by
  $w^{\mathrm{rev}}_i = w_{n+1-i}$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{80}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition rev_perm_ord : {perm 'I_n.+1} := perm (@rev_ord_inj n.+1).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The position-reversal involution $i \mapsto n - i$,
packaged as a permutation. \texttt{perm (h : injective f)} is the
constructor that turns an injective function into a
\texttt{\{perm T\}}.}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{85}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition rev_perm s : {perm 'I_n.+1} := rev_perm_ord * s.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$w^{\mathrm{rev}}(i) = s(n - i)$, the reversal of $s$
viewed at the level of positions.}

\begin{sidebar}{why \texttt{rev\_perm\_ord * s}, not \texttt{s * rev\_perm\_ord}?}
Mathcomp's group convention is \texttt{(s * t) i = t (s i)} (see
the lemma \texttt{permM}). With \texttt{rev\_perm := rev\_perm\_ord * s},
applying gives \texttt{(rev\_perm\_ord * s) i = s (rev\_perm\_ord i)
= s (n - i)}. This reverses \emph{positions} (Stanley's
$w^{\mathrm{rev}}_i = w_{n+1-i}$). Post-composing instead would
reverse \emph{values}, a different operation. Picking the wrong
side here would silently change the lemma's content; the choice
matches Stanley by direct check on a small example.
\end{sidebar}

\begin{lemma}[Descents of the reverse]
  \label{lem:is_descent_rev}
  Position $i$ is a descent of $w^{\mathrm{rev}}$ iff position $n - i$
  is \emph{not} a descent of $w$:
  \[
    i \in \descent(w^{\mathrm{rev}})
    \iff
    (n - i) \notin \descent(w).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{descent.v}{90}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma is_descent_rev s (i : 'I_n) :
  is_descent (rev_perm s) i = ~~ is_descent s (rev_ord i).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Position $i$ is a descent of $w^{\mathrm{rev}}$ iff
position $n - i$ is \emph{not} a descent of $w$. The
\texttt{\textasciitilde\textasciitilde} is boolean negation.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Direct calculation: $\mathtt{(rev\_perm\,s)\,j} =
s(n-j)$, and the comparison $s(n-i) > s(n-i-1)$ is the negation of
$s(n-i-1) > s(n-i)$. \qed

\section{Eulerian numbers}
\label{sec:eulerian}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Eulerian number]
  \label{def:eulerian}
  The \emph{Eulerian number} $\eulerianA(n, k)$ is the number of
  permutations of $\{1, \dots, n\}$ with exactly $k$ descents:
  \[
    \eulerianA(n, k) \;=\; \#\{\, w \in \Sn{n} : \des(w) = k \,\}.
  \]
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{eulerian.v}{14}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition eulerian (n k : nat) : nat :=
  #|[set s : {perm 'I_n.+1} | des s == k]|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\texttt{eulerian}\,n\,k = |\{s \in \Sn{n+1} : \des(s) = k\}|$.
The \texttt{==} is boolean equality on \texttt{nat}, and
\texttt{\#|\ldots|} is set cardinality.}

\noindent
\textbf{Off-by-one alert.} The Rocq parameter $n$ is ``permutation
size minus one'' (see the design-choice sidebar above). Concretely,
\texttt{eulerian n k} counts perms of $\Perm{\ord{n+1}}$, which are
Stanley's $\Sn{n+1}$. Hence
\[
  \texttt{eulerian } n\ k \;=\; \eulerianA(n+1, k).
\]
For instance, \texttt{eulerian 3 1} corresponds to Stanley's
$\eulerianA(4, 1) = 11$ (Stanley table p.~36).

\begin{computestrip}
Compute eulerian 0 0.    (* = 1     -- Stanley A(1,0) = 1 *)
Compute eulerian 1 0.    (* = 1     -- Stanley A(2,0) = 1 *)
Compute eulerian 1 1.    (* = 1     -- Stanley A(2,1) = 1 *)
Compute eulerian 2 0.    (* = 1     -- Stanley A(3,0) = 1 *)
Compute eulerian 2 1.    (* = 4     -- Stanley A(3,1) = 4 *)
Compute eulerian 2 2.    (* = 1     -- Stanley A(3,2) = 1 *)
\end{computestrip}

\noindent The numbers match Stanley's table on p.~36. Computing
\texttt{eulerian} on \texttt{\{perm 'I\_n.+1\}} requires enumerating
the set; for $n \geq 4$ the brute-force enumeration is slow, but
for the small cases above it returns immediately and the values
agree.

\begin{lemma}[Row sum]
  \label{lem:eulerian_row_sum}
  $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \eulerianA(n, k) = n!$
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{eulerian.v}{31}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma eulerian_row_sum_fact n :
  \sum_(k < n.+1) eulerian n k = n.+1`!.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\sum_{k=0}^{n} \eulerianA(n+1, k) = (n+1)!$. The
\texttt{`!} is mathcomp's factorial notation; \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(k\,<\,n.+1)}
sums over $k \in \{0, \dots, n\}$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} The summands partition $\Sn{n+1}$ by descent
count; the total is $|\Sn{n+1}| = (n+1)!$. \qed

\begin{lemma}[Out-of-range vanishing]
  \label{lem:eulerian_out_of_range}
  $\eulerianA(n, k) = 0$ for $k \geq n$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{eulerian.v}{34}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma eulerian_out_of_range n k :
  n < k -> eulerian n k = 0.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{If $k > n$ then $\eulerianA(n+1, k) = 0$, since perms of
length $n+1$ have at most $n$ descents.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} A permutation has at most $n$ descent positions
(Lemma~\ref{lem:des_le}); none can have $k > n$ descents. \qed

\begin{lemma}[The unique perm with no descents is the identity]
  \label{lem:des0_id}
  If $\des(w) = 0$ then $w = \mathrm{id}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{eulerian.v}{41}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma des0_id n (s : {perm 'I_n.+1}) : des s = 0 -> s = 1%g.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{If $\des(s) = 0$ then $s$ is the identity permutation.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} $\des(s) = 0$ means $s(i) < s(i+1)$ for every
adjacent pair $i$, so $s$ is strictly increasing. The only strictly
increasing function $\ord{n+1} \to \ord{n+1}$ is the identity. \qed

% =====================================================================
\section{The insert-max bijection}
\label{sec:insert_max}
% =====================================================================

A central tool for the Eulerian recurrence is the bijection that
inserts the maximum value $n+1$ at a chosen position into a
permutation of $\{1, \dots, n\}$.

\begin{definition}[Insert-max]
  \label{def:insert_max}
  For $t \in \Sn{n}$ and $p \in \{1, \dots, n+1\}$, let
  $\mathtt{insert\_max}(t, p) \in \Sn{n+1}$ be the permutation
  obtained by inserting the value $n+1$ at position $p$ in the
  one-line form of $t$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsourcerange{eulerian.v}{134}{157}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition insert_max_fun (i : 'I_n.+2) : 'I_n.+2 :=
  match unlift p i with
  | Some j => widen_ord (leqnSn _) (t j)
  | None => ord_max
  end.

Definition insert_max_perm : {perm 'I_n.+2} := perm insert_max_fun_inj.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{For $i \in \{0, \dots, n+1\}$: if $i = p$, output $n+1$
(the new max); otherwise, write $i = $ \texttt{lift p j} for the
unique $j \in \{0, \dots, n\}$ that skips $p$, and output $t(j)$
cast into the larger type.}

\begin{sidebar}{why \texttt{match unlift p i with}?}
The fundamental case-split for inserting at position $p$ is ``is
this position $p$ or not?''. Mathcomp encodes this as the
\texttt{option}-valued partial inverse \texttt{unlift p}: it
returns \texttt{None} exactly when $i = p$, and
\texttt{Some j} otherwise --- where $j : \ord{n+1}$ is the unique
witness of $i = $ \texttt{lift p j}. Carrying $j$ in the
\texttt{Some} branch (rather than recomputing it from $i$ and $p$)
avoids any arithmetic juggling: we get the correct
\texttt{'I\_n.+1} value with its bound proof for free, and can
apply $t$ directly. This is the universal idiom for constructing
permutations on \texttt{'I\_n.+2} from a permutation on
\texttt{'I\_n.+1} plus a special index.
\end{sidebar}

\begin{definition}[Extract-max]
  \label{def:extract_max}
  The inverse: given $\sigma \in \Sn{n+1}$ with $\sigma(p) = n+1$
  for some position $p$, remove that value and standardise to obtain
  $t \in \Sn{n}$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsourcerange{eulerian.v}{185}{203}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition extract_max_fun (j : 'I_n.+1) : 'I_n.+1 :=
  odflt j (unlift ord_max (s (lift p j))).

Definition extract_max_perm : {perm 'I_n.+1} := perm extract_max_fun_inj.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{For $j \in \{0, \dots, n\}$: look at $s$ at position $j$
of the original (skipping $p$); the result is some value in
$\{0, \dots, n+1\}$ that is not $n+1$ (because $\sigma(p) = n+1$
and $\sigma$ is a bijection); strip the high bit to land in
$\{0, \dots, n\}$. The \texttt{odflt j (...)} provides a default
that is provably unreachable.}

\begin{theorem}[Insert-max is a bijection]
  \label{thm:insert_max_bij}
  The map $(t, p) \mapsto \mathtt{insert\_max}(t, p)$ is a bijection
  $\Sn{n} \times \{1, \dots, n+1\} \to \Sn{n+1}$.
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{eulerian.v}{437}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma insert_max_perm_bij n :
  forall sigma : {perm 'I_n.+2},
    exists! tp : {perm 'I_n.+1} * 'I_n.+2,
      sigma = insert_max_perm tp.1 tp.2.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Every $\sigma \in \Sn{n+2}$ arises uniquely as
$\mathtt{insert\_max}(t, p)$ for some $(t, p) \in \Sn{n+1} \times \{0, \dots, n+1\}$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Given $\sigma$, the unique $p$ is
$\sigma^{-1}(n+1)$ and $t = \mathtt{extract\_max}(\sigma, p)$. The
two roundtrips are direct calculations. \qed

% =====================================================================
\section{Eulerian recurrence}
\label{sec:eulerian_rec}
% =====================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Eulerian recurrence]
  \label{thm:eulerian_rec}
  For $n \geq 1$ and $0 \leq k \leq n$,
  \[
    \eulerianA(n+1, k)
    \;=\;
    (k+1)\,\eulerianA(n, k) \;+\; (n - k + 1)\,\eulerianA(n, k-1).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{eulerian.v}{458}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem eulerian_rec n k :
  eulerian n.+1 k =
    (k.+1) * eulerian n k + (n.+1 - k) * eulerian n k.-1.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\eulerianA(n+2, k) = (k+1)\,\eulerianA(n+1, k) + (n+1 - k)\,\eulerianA(n+1, k-1)$.
The \texttt{n.+1\,-\,k} on the second term reads as ``$n+1-k$''
under our parameterisation; compare to Stanley's $(n+1-k)$ in
$A(n+2, k) = (k+1) A(n+1, k) + (n+1 - k)\,A(n+1, k-1)$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Using the insert-max bijection, classify each
$\sigma \in \Sn{n+1}$ by the position $p$ of $n+1$. There are
$k+1$ ascent positions and $n - k$ descent positions where inserting
$n+1$ preserves the descent count; inserting at the other $n - k + 1$
slots increases it by one. Summing recovers the recurrence. \qed

% =====================================================================
\section{The set-refined count $\beta$}
\label{sec:beta}
% =====================================================================

The descent count is a coarse invariant. The full descent
\emph{set} carries more information; the count of permutations with
a fixed descent set is a finer object.

\begin{definition}[$\beta$ count]
  \label{def:beta}
  For a subset $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n-1\}$, the count
  $\beps{n}{S}$ is the number of permutations of $\{1, \dots, n\}$
  whose descent set is exactly $S$:
  \[
    \beps{n}{S} \;=\; \#\{\, w \in \Sn{n} : \descent(w) = S \,\}.
  \]
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{beta.v}{19}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition beta (n : nat) (D : {set 'I_n}) : nat :=
  #|[set sigma : {perm 'I_n.+1} | descent_set sigma == D]|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\texttt{beta}\,n\,D = |\{\sigma \in \Sn{n+1} : \descent(\sigma) = D\}|$,
the number of perms of length $n+1$ with descent set exactly $D$.}

\noindent
\textbf{Off-by-one alert.} As with \texttt{eulerian}, the Rocq
parameter $n$ is ``perm size minus one.'' So
\texttt{beta n D} for $D : \Set{\ord{n}}$ equals Stanley's
$\beps{n+1}{D}$, with $D$ interpreted as a subset of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$
that corresponds to Stanley's 1-indexed $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$.

\begin{lemma}[$\beta$ partitions Eulerian]
  \label{lem:beta_eulerian}
  Summing $\beps{n}{S}$ over all $S$ of cardinality $k$ recovers the
  Eulerian number:
  \[
    \sum_{|S| = k} \beps{n}{S} \;=\; \eulerianA(n, k).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{beta.v}{124}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma beta_eulerian n k :
  \sum_(D : {set 'I_n} | #|D| == k) beta D = eulerian n k.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\sum_{D \subseteq \{0, \dots, n-1\},\ |D| = k} \beps{n+1}{D} = \eulerianA(n+1, k)$.
The big-op syntax \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(D\,:\,\{set 'I\_n\}\,|\,\#|D|\,==\,k)}
sums over all sets $D$ of cardinality exactly $k$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} The sets $\{\sigma : \descent(\sigma) = D\}$ for
$D$ ranging over subsets of cardinality $k$ partition the set of
permutations with $k$ descents. The cardinalities sum. \qed

\begin{lemma}[$\beta$ is reversal-invariant]
  \label{lem:beta_rev}
  For $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n-1\}$, let $S^{\mathrm{rev}} =
  \{n - i : i \in S\}$. Then
  $\beps{n}{S^{\mathrm{rev}}} = \beps{n}{S^{\mathrm{c}}}$,
  where $S^{\mathrm{c}}$ is the complement in $\{1, \dots, n-1\}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{beta.v}{140}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma beta_rev n (D : {set 'I_n}) :
  beta [set rev_ord i | i in D] = beta (~: D).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\beps{n+1}{\{n-1-i : i \in D\}} = \beps{n+1}{\overline{D}}$.
The set comprehension \texttt{[set rev\_ord i | i in D]} is the
image of $D$ under $i \mapsto n-1-i$; \texttt{\textasciitilde:\,D}
is the complement of $D$ in \texttt{'I\_n}.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Reversal $w \mapsto w^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is a
bijection on $\Sn{n}$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:is_descent_rev} it maps
descent set $D$ to $\{n - i : i \notin D\}$, which is the
complement of $\{n - i : i \in D\}$. Counting on both sides
matches. \qed

\chapter{The $q$-analogues}
\label{ch:qanalogues}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes the $q$-extensions of \S1.4: the
$q$-factorial $[n]_q!$ as the inversion generating function over
$\Sn{n}$, and the bivariate $q$-Eulerian polynomial
$\eulerianA_n(q, t)$ as the joint generating function for
$(\maj, \des)$. The two specializations $t = 1$ and $q = 1$ recover
$[n]_q!$ and the classical Eulerian polynomial respectively.

% =====================================================================
\section{$q$-integers and the $q$-factorial}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[$q$-integer]
  $[n]_q = 1 + q + q^2 + \cdots + q^{n-1}$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{qfact.v}{25}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition q_int (n : nat) : {poly int} := \sum_(i < n) 'X^i.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$[n]_q$ as a polynomial in the formal indeterminate
\texttt{'X}, with integer coefficients. \texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\,<\,n)\,'X\textasciicircum i}
sums $X^i$ for $i \in \{0, \dots, n-1\}$.}

\begin{definition}[$q$-factorial]
  $[n]_q! = [1]_q \cdot [2]_q \cdots [n]_q$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{qfact.v}{26}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition q_fact (n : nat) : {poly int} := \prod_(k < n.+1) q_int k.+1.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\texttt{q\_fact}\,n = [1]_q \cdot [2]_q \cdots [n+1]_q
= [n+1]_q!$. The off-by-one is the same as in
\texttt{eulerian}: our parameter is ``size minus one,'' so
$\texttt{q\_fact}\,n = [n+1]_q!$ in Stanley's notation.}

\begin{theorem}[$\inv$-generating function for $\Sn{n}$]
  $\sum_{w \in \Sn{n}} q^{\inv(w)} = [n]_q!$
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{qfact.v}{188}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem inv_q_fact n :
  \sum_(σ : {perm 'I_n.+1}) 'X^(inv σ) = q_fact n :> {poly int}.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The polynomial $\sum_{\sigma \in \Sn{n+1}} X^{\inv(\sigma)}$
is exactly $[n+1]_q!$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Induction on $n$: at each step, classify
permutations of $\Sn{n+2}$ by where the maximum was inserted (using
\texttt{insert\_max\_perm\_bij}); inserting at position $p$ adds
exactly $n+1 - p$ inversions, so the sum factors as
$[n+1]_q! \cdot [n+2]_q$. \qed

\begin{theorem}[$\maj$-generating function via Foata]
  $\sum_{w \in \Sn{n}} q^{\maj(w)} = [n]_q!$
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{qfact.v}{218}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem maj_q_fact n :
  \sum_(σ : {perm 'I_n.+1}) 'X^(maj σ) = q_fact n :> {poly int}.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Same identity as \texttt{inv\_q\_fact} but for $\maj$
instead of $\inv$ --- a direct corollary of MacMahon's
equidistribution.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} Reindex the $\maj$-sum along Foata's bijection:
$\sum_\sigma X^{\maj(\sigma)} = \sum_\sigma X^{\inv(\mathrm{foata}(\sigma))}$
which equals $[n+1]_q!$ by the previous theorem. \qed

% =====================================================================
\section{The bivariate $q$-Eulerian polynomial}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[$q$-Eulerian polynomial]
  $\eulerianA_n(q, t) = \sum_{w \in \Sn{n}} q^{\maj(w)} \cdot t^{\des(w)}$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{qeul.v}{37}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition q_eul_pol (n : nat) : {poly {poly int}} :=
  \sum_(σ : {perm 'I_n.+1}) qpow (maj σ) * 'X^(des σ).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\eulerianA_{n+1}(q, t) = \sum_{\sigma \in \Sn{n+1}}
q^{\maj(\sigma)} t^{\des(\sigma)}$. Bivariate: outer indeterminate
\texttt{'X} is $t$, inner (under \texttt{qpow}) is $q$.}

\begin{sidebar}{why bivariate \texttt{\{poly \{poly int\}\}}?}
The natural type for ``polynomial in $t$ with coefficients
polynomials in $q$'' is the iterated polynomial ring
\texttt{\{poly \{poly int\}\}}. Mathcomp's polynomial library is
fully composable: \texttt{\{poly R\}} is itself a ring (in fact a
commutative ring if $R$ is), so \texttt{\{poly \{poly int\}\}}
inherits all the algebra. The trade-off is that the syntax is
heavier --- the constant-poly embedding \texttt{\_\%:P}, the
notation \texttt{qpow k} (for the inner $q^k$ embedded as an outer
constant), and \texttt{horner\_eval} for substitution all need to
be juggled. We name them in advance to keep the recurrence
readable.
\end{sidebar}

\begin{theorem}[Specialize $t := 1$]
  $\eulerianA_n(q, 1) = [n]_q!$
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{qeul.v}{44}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem q_eul_pol_t1 n :
  (q_eul_pol n).[1] = q_fact n.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Evaluating $\eulerianA_{n+1}(q, t)$ at $t = 1$ gives
$\sum_\sigma q^{\maj(\sigma)} \cdot 1^{\des(\sigma)} =
\sum_\sigma q^{\maj(\sigma)} = [n+1]_q!$ (the previous theorem).}

\begin{definition}[Classical Eulerian polynomial]
  $\eulerianA_n(t) = \sum_{k \geq 0} \eulerianA(n, k) \cdot t^k$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{qeul.v}{58}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition eul_pol (n : nat) : {poly int} :=
  \sum_(k < n.+1) (eulerian n k)%:R * 'X^k.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\texttt{eul\_pol}\,n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \eulerianA(n+1, k) \cdot t^k$
(the off-by-one inherited from \texttt{eulerian}).}

\begin{theorem}[Specialize $q := 1$]
  $\eulerianA_n(1, t) = \eulerianA_n(t)$, the classical Eulerian
  polynomial.
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{qeul.v}{84}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem q_eul_pol_q1 n :
  q1_subst (q_eul_pol n) = eul_pol n.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Substituting $q = 1$ in each coefficient of $\eulerianA_{n+1}(q, t)$
recovers the classical Eulerian polynomial. The substitution is
implemented by \texttt{q1\_subst} = \texttt{map\_poly (horner\_eval 1)},
which evaluates each inner $q$-polynomial at $q = 1$.}

\chapter{Longest alternating subsequence}
\label{ch:altsub}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes Stanley \S1.6.2: the longest alternating
subsequence statistic $\mathrm{as}(w)$, with the closed-form
characterization
\[
  \mathrm{as}(w) \;=\; \mathrm{turn\_count}(w) + 2,
\]
where the right-hand side is two more than the number of
``turning points'' (positions where the direction of $w$ changes).

% =====================================================================
\section{Turning points}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Turning point]
  Position $i$ is a \emph{turning point} of $w$ if the direction at
  position $i$ differs from the direction at position $i+1$:
  exactly one of $\{i, i+1\}$ is a descent position.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{41}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition is_turn s (i : 'I_n) : bool :=
  is_descent s (widen_ord (leqnSn _) i) (+) is_descent s (lift ord0 i).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\mathrm{is\_turn}\,s\,i$ is the boolean XOR of two adjacent
\texttt{is\_descent} indicators. For
$s : \Perm{\ord{n+2}}$ and $i : \ord{n}$, the two arguments
of XOR are descent slots $i$ and $i+1$ (both in $\ord{n+1}$, via
the same widen+lift idiom as Chapter~\ref{ch:descents}).}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{44}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition turn_count s : nat :=
  #|[set i : 'I_n | is_turn s i]|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\mathrm{turn\_count}(s) = $ number of turning positions.}

% =====================================================================
\section{Alternating subsequences}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Alternating sequence of naturals]
  A seq $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k$ is \emph{alternating} if
  $x_1 < x_2 > x_3 < \cdots$ or $x_1 > x_2 < x_3 > \cdots$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{71}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition is_alt (xs : seq nat) : bool :=
  match xs with
  | [::] => true
  | [:: _] => true
  | x :: y :: xs' =>
      ((x < y) && alt_aux false y xs') || ((y < x) && alt_aux true y xs')
  end.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Empty and singleton seqs are alternating. Otherwise we try
both starting directions: ``up first'' (\texttt{x < y} then
\texttt{alt\_aux false}) or ``down first'' (\texttt{y < x} then
\texttt{alt\_aux true}); $\mathrm{is\_alt}$ holds iff either branch
succeeds. The auxiliary \texttt{alt\_aux} alternates the boolean
parity at each step.}

\begin{definition}[Permutation as a seq, subseq at chosen positions]
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{84}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition perm_seq n (s : {perm 'I_n.+2}) : seq nat :=
  [seq val (s i) | i <- enum 'I_n.+2].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The one-line form of $s$: the seq $[s(0); s(1); \ldots; s(n+1)]$
of natural numbers.}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{97}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition pick_seq n (s : {perm 'I_n.+2}) (I : {set 'I_n.+2}) : seq nat :=
  [seq val (s j) | j <- sort (fun a b => val a <= val b) (enum I)].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{For an index set $I \subseteq \{0, \dots, n+1\}$, sort the
positions in $I$ in increasing order and pick the corresponding
values from $s$. This is ``the subsequence of $s$ at positions $I$,
in order.''}

% =====================================================================
\section{The two definitions of \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{as}$}{as}}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Bijective form: $\mathrm{as\_perm\_max}$]
  $\mathrm{as\_perm\_max}(s) = \max \{ |I| : \mathrm{pick\_seq}(s, I) \text{ is alternating}\}$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{105}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition as_perm_max n (s : {perm 'I_n.+2}) : nat :=
  \max_(I : {set 'I_n.+2} | is_alt (pick_seq s I)) #|I|.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The maximum cardinality over all index sets $I$ whose
sorted-value picked subseq is alternating. This is the geometric
``length of the longest alternating subseq'' definition.}

\begin{definition}[Closed-form: $\mathrm{as\_perm}$]
  $\mathrm{as\_perm}(s) = \mathrm{turn\_count}(s) + 2$.
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{109}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition as_perm n (s : {perm 'I_n.+2}) : nat := (turn_count s).+2.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Two more than the number of turning positions. This is
Stanley's \emph{closed-form} characterization, taken here as a
definition; the equivalence with the bijective form is the
headline theorem below.}

% =====================================================================
\section{The headline equivalence}
% =====================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Stanley's characterization, \S1.6.2]
  \label{thm:as_perm_max_eq}
  For every $s \in \Sn{n+2}$,
  $\mathrm{as\_perm\_max}(s) = \mathrm{turn\_count}(s) + 2$.
\end{theorem}

\rocqsource{altsub.v}{2114}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Theorem as_perm_max_eq s : as_perm_max s = (turn_count s).+2.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The longest alternating subsequence of $s$ has length
exactly $\mathrm{turn\_count}(s) + 2$.}

\begin{sidebar}{why this took five sessions}
The proof has two halves --- $\geq$ (existence of a long alt
subseq) and $\leq$ (no longer one exists). The lower bound is
constructive: pick the boundary endpoints plus one position per
turning point. The upper bound is where the difficulty was: the
naive ``leftmost turn in $[\sigma_i, \sigma_{i+2})$'' injection
from sign-flips of the picked subseq to turning positions of $s$
\emph{is not injective} --- adjacent sign-flips can share a single
turning window. The proof in \texttt{altsub.v} pivots to a global
bound via the seq-level statistic
\texttt{flip\_count}, which is monotone under taking subseqs:
$\texttt{flip\_count}(\texttt{sign\_seq}(\texttt{pick\_seq}\,s\,I))
\leq \texttt{flip\_count}(\texttt{sign\_seq}(\texttt{perm\_seq}\,s))
= \texttt{turn\_count}(s)$. The detour is recorded in
\texttt{docs/plans/ALTSUB\_PLAN.md}.
\end{sidebar}

\chapter{Toggle action and Stanley's Corollary 1.6.5}
\label{ch:toggle}
% =====================================================================

This chapter formalizes Stanley \S1.6.3: the toggle action on
descent sets, the $\omega$-map, and the headline corollary that
the alternating descent set strictly maximizes $\beps{n}{S}$ ---
the central theorem of this project.

% =====================================================================
\section{Symmetric difference and single-position toggle}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Symmetric difference, single-position toggle]
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{beta_omega.v}{25}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition sym_diff (n : nat) (D E : {set 'I_n}) : {set 'I_n} :=
  (D :\: E) :|: (E :\: D).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Standard set-theoretic symmetric difference $D \triangle E$.}

\rocqsource{beta_omega.v}{28}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition toggle_at (n : nat) (D : {set 'I_n}) (i : 'I_n) : {set 'I_n} :=
  sym_diff D [set i].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\mathrm{toggle\_at}(D, i) = D \triangle \{i\}$. Adds $i$
if absent, removes it if present.}

% =====================================================================
\section{The $\omega$-map}
% =====================================================================

For a descent set $D \subseteq \{1, \dots, n-1\}$ in Stanley's
$\Sn{n}$, $\omega(D)$ records the positions where adjacent
membership in $D$ changes.

\rocqsource{beta_omega.v}{57}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition omega_set (n : nat) (D : {set 'I_n.+1}) : {set 'I_n} :=
  [set k : 'I_n | (widen_ord (leqnSn n) k \in D) != (lift ord0 k \in D)].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$k \in \omega(D)$ iff membership of position $k$ in $D$
\emph{differs} from membership of position $k+1$ in $D$. The two
membership tests use the now-familiar widen+lift idiom to view $k$
and $k+1$ inside the larger type.}

% =====================================================================
\section{The alternating descent set}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Alternating descent pattern]
  $D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n = \{1, 3, 5, \dots\} \cap \{1, \dots, n-1\}$
  (Stanley's 1-indexed odd positions).
\end{definition}

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{31}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition alt_desc_set (n : nat) : {set 'I_n} :=
  [set i : 'I_n | ~~ odd i].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The set of \emph{even-indexed} positions $\{0, 2, 4, \dots\}$
of $\ord{n}$. Under our 0-indexing, this matches Stanley's
1-indexed odd positions $\{1, 3, 5, \dots\}$.}

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{40}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition set_is_alt (n : nat) (D : {set 'I_n}) : bool :=
  [forall i : 'I_n, [forall j : 'I_n,
     (val j == (val i).+1) ==> ((i \in D) != (j \in D))]].
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{$\mathrm{set\_is\_alt}(D)$: every consecutive pair
$(i, i+1)$ in $\ord{n}$ has differing membership in $D$.
Equivalently, $D$ is either $D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n$ or its complement
(lemma \texttt{set\_is\_alt\_classify} below).}

% =====================================================================
\section{Value-complement and \texorpdfstring{$\beta$}{β} symmetry}
% =====================================================================

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{128}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Definition compl_perm n (s : {perm 'I_n.+1}) : {perm 'I_n.+1} :=
  s * rev_perm_ord n.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The value-complement of $s$: $\mathrm{compl\_perm}(s)\,i = n - s(i)$.
Defined by post-composition with the value-reversal involution.}

\begin{lemma}[Descent set under value-complement]
  $\descent(\mathrm{compl\_perm}(s)) = \overline{\descent(s)}$
  (the complement in $\ord{n}$).
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{160}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma descent_set_compl n (s : {perm 'I_n.+1}) :
  descent_set (compl_perm s) = ~: descent_set s.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Value-complementing every value flips ascents into
descents and vice-versa, so the descent set goes to its complement.}

\begin{lemma}[$\beta$ is complement-invariant]
  \label{lem:beta_compl}
  $\beps{n}{D} = \beps{n}{\overline{D}}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{166}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma beta_compl n (D : {set 'I_n}) : beta D = beta (~: D).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{The number of perms with descent set $D$ equals the number
with descent set $\overline{D}$, by the bijection
$s \mapsto \mathrm{compl\_perm}(s)$.}

\begin{lemma}[Set-alt classification]
  $\mathrm{set\_is\_alt}(D)$ holds iff $D = D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n$ or
  $D = \overline{D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n}$.
\end{lemma}

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{182}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma set_is_alt_classify n (D : {set 'I_n}) :
  set_is_alt D = (D == alt_desc_set n) || (D == ~: alt_desc_set n).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Exactly two sets in $\ord{n}$ are set-alternating: the
alternating pattern itself and its complement. This justifies
``\emph{the} alternating descent set'' modulo the two-flavour
ambiguity.}

% =====================================================================
\section{Stanley's Proposition 1.6.4}
% =====================================================================

\begin{proposition}[$\omega$-strictness, Stanley Prop 1.6.4]
  \label{prop:omega_proper_beta_lt}
  If $\omega(D) \subsetneq \omega(E)$ then $\beps{n}{D} < \beps{n}{E}$.
\end{proposition}

\rocqsource{perm_seq_bridge.v}{540}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma omega_proper_beta_lt : forall m (D E : {set 'I_m.+1}),
  omega_set D \proper omega_set E ->
  beta D < beta E.
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{Strictly enlarging the $\omega$-image strictly increases
$\beta$. The proof goes through a deep ``cd-index'' machinery
(min-max trees, $\psi_i$ operators) developed in Stanley's
chapter and formalized in this project's \texttt{psi\_*.v} files;
that material is not reproduced here.}

% =====================================================================
\section{The headline: Stanley's Corollary 1.6.5}
% =====================================================================

\begin{corollary}[Alternating set strictly maximizes $\beta$, Stanley Cor 1.6.5]
  \label{cor:beta_alt_max}
  For every set $D \subseteq \{1, \dots, n-1\}$ that is \emph{not}
  alternating,
  \[
    \beps{n}{D} \;<\; \Eul_n,
  \]
  where $\Eul_n = \beps{n}{D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n}$ is the Euler number.
\end{corollary}

\rocqsource{beta_swap.v}{273}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rocq]
Lemma beta_alt_max n (D : {set 'I_n}) :
  ~~ set_is_alt D -> beta D < beta (alt_desc_set n).
\end{lstlisting}

\decoded{If $D$ is not set-alternating, then $\beta(D)$ is strictly
smaller than $\beta(D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n)$ --- the alternating
descent set is the \emph{unique} (up to complement) maximizer of
$\beta$.}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} If $D$ is not alternating then there exist
adjacent positions $i, i+1$ both in $D$ or both not. Toggling at
$i$ (or at a fitting nearby position) strictly enlarges
$\omega(D)$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:omega_proper_beta_lt}
gives a chain of strict $\beta$-increases that terminates at
$D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n$. (The full chain construction uses
\texttt{block\_left}/\texttt{block\_right} from
\texttt{beta\_omega.v}.) \qed

\noindent This is the headline theorem of the entire project. To
verify axiom-freeness:

\begin{verbatim}
echo 'From mathcomp_eulerian Require Import beta_swap.
      Print Assumptions beta_alt_max.' \
  | rocq top -R . mathcomp_eulerian
\end{verbatim}

\noindent prints \texttt{Closed under the global context}.

\chapter{Andr\'e's reflection method (partial)}
\label{ch:reflection}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Status.} This chapter records a \emph{partial}
formalization. The headline recurrence $2 \Eul_{n+2} = \sum_k
\binom{n+1}{k} \Eul_k \Eul_{n+1-k}$ is proven modulo one named
\texttt{Admitted} lemma, in the file
\texttt{experimental/reflection.v} which is \emph{out of the active
build chain} (excluded from \texttt{\_CoqProject}). The companion
document \texttt{docs/internal/PHASE\_C\_RETROSPECTIVE.md} captures
the eight sessions of work, the structural obstacle, and a concrete
$\sim$510 LOC discharge plan for any future contributor.

% =====================================================================
\section{Euler numbers}
% =====================================================================

\begin{definition}[Euler numbers]
  $\Eul_{n+1} = \beps{n+1}{D_{\mathrm{alt}}^n}$ (the count of
  alternating permutations of $\Sn{n+1}$). Set $\Eul_0 = 1$.
\end{definition}

\noindent (No \texttt{rocqsource} block since the file is out of the
build chain.)

% =====================================================================
\section{The headline recurrence}
% =====================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Andr\'e recurrence, modulo one admit]
  For every $n \geq 0$,
  \[
    2 \Eul_{n+2} \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} \binom{n+1}{k} \, \Eul_k \, \Eul_{n+1-k}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\noindent The formal statement \texttt{euler\_rec} in
\texttt{experimental/reflection.v} (line 1705) is derived in one
line from \texttt{two\_eulerA\_split} (already proven) and an
admitted lemma:

\[
  \texttt{sum\_set\_is\_alt\_eq\_andre\_sum} \;:\;
  \sum_{t} \sum_{p} \mathrm{set\_is\_alt}(\descent(\mathrm{insert\_max}(t, p)))
  \;=\; \sum_{k} \binom{n+1}{k} \Eul_k \Eul_{n+1-k}.
\]

\noindent The base case $n = 0$ ($2\Eul_2 = 2$) is proven
unconditionally in the same file
(\texttt{euler\_rec\_n0\_direct}); cases $n = 1, 2$ inherit the
admit transitively.

\begin{remark}[The structural obstacle]
The combinatorial heart of the admit is the boundary descent at
the position of the inserted maximum. After partitioning
$\sigma \in \Sn{n+2}$ by the position $p$ of the max and the value
set $L$ left of it, the descent at slot $p-1$ depends on
$(L, s_L, s_R)$ \emph{jointly}: specifically on whether the
largest left value (selected by $s_L$) exceeds the smallest right
value (selected by $s_R$). This couples the three components, so
$[\descent(\sigma) = E]$ does not factor into independent
$s_L$- and $s_R$-conditions. The classical proof absorbs the
coupling via the factor of $2$ on the LHS (sum over
\texttt{alt} and \texttt{\textasciitilde:alt}), but the formal
counterpart turns out to be a 4-to-1 reduction, not the 2-to-1
fibration suggested by the English proof. The retrospective
explains in detail why estimates for this discharge consistently
underran by a factor of $\sim$2--3.
\end{remark}

\chapter{Glossary of mathcomp primitives}
\label{app:glossary}
% =====================================================================

A quick-reference companion to Chapter~\ref{ch:primer}. Entries are
ordered roughly by first appearance in the chapter; each gives the
mathcomp source file, a one-line gloss, and an example.

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{'I\_n}] (\texttt{fintype.v}) The finite type of
    natural numbers strictly less than $n$. Inhabitant: a pair
    \texttt{(i, proof of i\,<\,n)}. Cardinality $n$.

  \item[\texttt{ord0\,:\,'I\_n.+1}] (\texttt{fintype.v}) The value
    $0$, with its proof of $0 < n+1$.

  \item[\texttt{ord\_max\,:\,'I\_n.+1}] (\texttt{fintype.v}) The
    value $n$, the largest element.

  \item[\texttt{val\,i\,:\,nat}] (\texttt{eqtype.v}) Strips an
    ordinal back to its underlying natural number.

  \item[\texttt{widen\_ord (h\,:\,m\,\textbackslash leq\,n) i}]
    (\texttt{fintype.v}) Casts \texttt{i\,:\,'I\_m} to \texttt{'I\_n}
    keeping the same numerical value. Pure cast.

  \item[\texttt{lift (p\,:\,'I\_n.+1) j}] (\texttt{fintype.v})
    Casts \texttt{j\,:\,'I\_n} to \texttt{'I\_n.+1} ``skipping''
    the value $p$. With $p = $ \texttt{ord0}, this is $j \mapsto j+1$.

  \item[\texttt{unlift p i\,:\,option 'I\_n}] (\texttt{fintype.v})
    Partial inverse of \texttt{lift p}. Returns \texttt{None} when
    $i = p$, else \texttt{Some j} with $i = $ \texttt{lift p j}.

  \item[\texttt{rev\_ord i}] (\texttt{fintype.v}) The involution
    $i \mapsto n - 1 - i$ on \texttt{'I\_n}.

  \item[\texttt{\{set T\}}] (\texttt{finset.v}) The type of finite
    subsets of \texttt{T} (a finite type), with extensional equality.

  \item[\texttt{[set i\,:\,T\,|\,P i]}] (\texttt{finset.v}) Set
    comprehension: \texttt{\{i\,:\,T\,|\,P i\}}.

  \item[\texttt{[set f i\,|\,i in S]}] (\texttt{finset.v}) Image of
    set $S$ under function $f$.

  \item[\texttt{\#|S|}] (\texttt{fintype.v}) Cardinality of a finite
    set or predicate.

  \item[\texttt{i \textbackslash in S}] (\texttt{finset.v})
    Membership test, returning \texttt{bool}.

  \item[\texttt{\textasciitilde:\,S}] (\texttt{finset.v}) Complement
    of $S$ in its ambient finite type.

  \item[\texttt{\{perm T\}}] (\texttt{perm.v}) Type of bijections
    \texttt{T\,\textbackslash to T} for \texttt{T} a finite type.
    Inhabitant: a function plus its proof of injectivity.

  \item[\texttt{1\%g}] (\texttt{fingroup.v}) Group identity. For
    \texttt{\{perm T\}}, the identity permutation.

  \item[\texttt{(s * t)\%g}] (\texttt{fingroup.v}) Group
    multiplication. For perms: \texttt{(s * t) i = t (s i)}; see
    lemma \texttt{permM}.

  \item[\texttt{perm (h\,:\,injective f)}] (\texttt{perm.v})
    Constructor: turn an injective function into a \texttt{\{perm T\}}.

  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\,:\,T) f i}] (\texttt{bigop.v})
    Sum of \texttt{f i} over every \texttt{i\,:\,T}.

  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\,<\,n) f i}] (\texttt{bigop.v})
    Same, indexed by \texttt{i\,:\,'I\_n}.

  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\,\textbackslash in\,S) f i}]
    (\texttt{bigop.v}) Sum over the elements of an explicit set
    \texttt{S}.

  \item[\texttt{\textbackslash sum\_(i\,|\,P i) f i}]
    (\texttt{bigop.v}) Sum over $i$ satisfying boolean filter $P$.
    The vertical bar is a filter, not disjunction.

  \item[\texttt{partition\_big f xpredT}] (\texttt{bigop.v})
    Rewrite a sum as a nested sum partitioned by the value of $f$.

  \item[\texttt{n.+1}, \texttt{n.+2}, \texttt{n.-1}] (\texttt{ssrnat.v})
    Successor, double-successor, predecessor on \texttt{nat}. Read
    as $n+1$, $n+2$, $n-1$.

  \item[\texttt{\textasciitilde\textasciitilde\,b}] Boolean negation.

  \item[\texttt{a\,==\,b}] (\texttt{eqtype.v}) Decidable equality
    on an \texttt{eqType}, returning \texttt{bool}. Distinct from
    propositional \texttt{=}; bridged by lemma \texttt{eqP}.

  \item[\texttt{Section X.\,Variable n.\,\ldots\,End X.}] (Rocq)
    Sections introduce parameters that are auto-generalised on
    \texttt{End}. Inside the section, \texttt{n} reads as a fixed
    natural; outside, every definition is implicitly parameterised.
\end{description}
